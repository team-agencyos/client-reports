2. Why the notifications may not be reaching your inbox

What the email log and your domain settings show
What we found
  • Your mail server accepted every notification since September 15 for mitch@, ray@, zohar@ and sarah@. We can see the exact second each one was accepted.
  • One real email fault: stacy@supplyvelocity.com has refused every notification since at least September 15. That usually means a full mailbox or a suspended account. Someone at Supply Velocity should check it.
  • What we cannot see is whether Google then put each email in your inbox or in Spam. That happens inside your Google Workspace.
  • Spam is the most likely answer. The notifications go out in Sarah's name but through an outside email service, your domain has no SPF record (one of the two checks Google uses to trust a sender), and the six messages were sales pitches. That combination usually ends up in Spam.
Two minutes that settle it
  1. In Gmail, search all mail including Spam for: New Request for Consultation submission
  2. Tell us where the September 22 and 23 ones are: Inbox, Spam or nowhere.
  3. Forward one of them to hello@outriderdigital.com as an attachment. Its headers show us exactly how Google judged it.
If they are in Spam, marking one as "Not spam" keeps the rest in your inbox from then on. Either way we are moving the notifications to a dedicated, properly authenticated sending address, adding the missing SPF record to your domain, and copying every submission to us so a missed one is caught on our side.

4. Is a three-week gap unusual?

Weekly pattern since August
What we found
  • Yes, it is. Real enquiries ran at one to four a week from early August to the first week of September, then stopped. People selling to you kept coming at two to four a week.
  • The traffic that feeds the forms did not fall. Visits to the contact page were higher in the second half of September (61) than the first (49). Visits from Google in the United States held at 47 to 62 a week all month, level with August and well above September last year.
  • Nothing changed on the website. The form code has not been touched since before August; the only September change was an optional 'How did you find us?' field on September 8, and submissions continued after it.

Clicks and impressions, weekly
What we found
  • Impressions roughly halved while clicks held, and here is where the impressions went: broad searches like 'supply chain consulting', where the site sat on page four or five and almost never earned a click. Losing those cost no visits.
