Where visibility is thinnest

Gap 1

Atlanta in Claude

Claude did not name Supply Velocity for any Atlanta question. The fix is the Atlanta page itself plus the third-party mentions that engine draws on.

Gap 2

Philadelphia in ChatGPT

ChatGPT did not name Supply Velocity for any Philadelphia question, and it is the weakest engine overall. Same remedy: a stronger local page and outside references.

Claude did not name Supply Velocity for Kansas City. Chicago, St Louis, Denver and Indianapolis are solid across every engine that completed.

What the table shows

Organic traffic is the part of your visits that comes from people finding you on Google. September was the best month since July: sessions up a seventh on August and search clicks up a fifth on matched days, while impressions fell by more than a third. Fewer impressions with more clicks means Google stopped showing the site on results far down the page, where nobody clicked anyway, and kept showing it where people do.

What is driving it

Searches for Supply Velocity by name grew and the homepage took most of the extra clicks. The warehouse bin location guide and its PDF, the supply chain assessment page and the Chicago consulting page all gained, and the site held first place for supply chain assessment and second for S&OP consultants. The table below lists the pages and the searches that moved.

Where it is still soft

The boutique consulting firms guide, which slipped in August, eased a little further and now earns a handful of clicks from tens of thousands of impressions; its refresh is in the October batch. The six pages published at the end of August are indexed but only the Miami page has drawn search visits so far, which is normal in their second month.

Where the visits come from

Search visits from the ten target markets themselves are still a handful each month, as the market table at the end of this section shows, while the AI engines already name Supply Velocity for buyers in most of those same cities. That gap is the opportunity the local pages and the content batch are built to close, and it is the number to watch over the next quarter rather than the monthly total.

What is working elsewhere

AI engines already name Supply Velocity in most buyer questions, as the section above shows, and the inventory optimisation and S&OP pages hold top-ten positions for their terms. Brand searches growing while non-brand impressions are trimmed is the shape of a site Google trusts more, not less.

What we are doing

Refreshing the two older guides for 2026, publishing the October content batch, strengthening the Atlanta, Philadelphia and Kansas City pages that the AI scan flagged, and publishing the Assessment page under Services once Ray's notes are in. No new backlinks were placed in September while we reviewed the mix of sites; the next batch is being scoped. We expect the search numbers to keep firming through October.
