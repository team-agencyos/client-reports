What the table shows

Organic traffic is the part of your visits that comes from people finding you on Google. September was the best month since July: sessions up a seventh on August and search clicks up a fifth on matched days, while impressions fell by more than a third. Fewer impressions with more clicks means Google stopped showing the site on results far down the page, where nobody clicked anyway, and kept showing it where people do.

What is driving it

Searches for Supply Velocity by name grew and the homepage took most of the extra clicks. The warehouse bin location guide and its PDF, the supply chain assessment page and the Chicago consulting page all gained, and the site held first place for supply chain assessment and second for S&OP consultants. Where it is still soft

The boutique consulting firms guide, which slipped in August, eased a little further and now earns a handful of clicks from tens of thousands of impressions; its refresh is in the October batch. The six pages published at the end of August are indexed but only the Miami page has drawn search visits so far, which is normal in their second month.

What is working elsewhere

AI engines already name Supply Velocity in most buyer questions, as the section above shows, and the inventory optimisation and S&OP pages hold top-ten positions for their terms. Brand searches growing while non-brand impressions are trimmed is the shape of a site Google trusts more, not less.

What we are doing

Refreshing the two older guides for 2026, publishing the October content batch, strengthening the Atlanta, Philadelphia and Kansas City pages that the AI scan flagged, and taking the Assessment page live once Ray's review is in. No new backlinks were placed in September while we reviewed the mix of sites; the next batch is being scoped. We expect the search numbers to keep firming through October.
