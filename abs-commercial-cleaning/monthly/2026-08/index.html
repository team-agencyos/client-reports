August ran in four stages, and they were not evenly sized. The first ten days were handover preparation — checking the new design against search best practice, and getting the domain records, the code repository and the hosting ownership into your hands before anything moved. The middle of the month was a single piece of work that shaped everything after it: a full pre-launch audit of the site, which found the mismatched addresses, the empty FAQ sections, the missing logos and the per-location maps. Most of the corrections further down this list exist because that audit found them first. The switch itself happened in the third week, and the list under it is long because replacing a website is really a stack of small guarantees: a redirect carried over for every old address, the media library and the history of past form submissions pulled off the old host, the URL list circulated for review beforehand, the old hosting left running for a month afterwards as a fallback, and the content pass that settled what the site is allowed to claim about your business. All of it sits in a repository and hosting account your business owns. The final week was expansion: twelve pages the site did not have before — five facility types, four services that were missing entirely, and city versions of those services — plus the menus, footers and phone numbers that now follow whichever city a visitor has selected. The month ahead is intended to turn the new site into local visibility: optimising your Google Business Profile, local keyword research, and a clean search baseline now that the rebuild is the version Google sees. That is our sequencing rather than a set of promised dates.

Read this month's comparison carefully, because it is not like-for-like. The old WordPress site and the new build each served part of August, so visits, bounce rate and recorded enquiries are measured across two different websites rather than one website over time. Visits fell against July and recorded enquiries fell further. Some of that is the switch itself, and some of it may be measurement: the system behind the quote and contact forms was rebuilt as part of the move, and a form that delivers correctly can still fail to register as a conversion if tracking has not been re-attached. We are verifying enquiry capture end to end on the new build before drawing any conclusion from this figure, and we would rather tell you that now than present a clean-looking number we do not yet trust. The first honest read on traffic comes next month, with a full period on a single site.

Search is where the month is unambiguous. Impressions, clicks and click-through rate all rose, and average position improved — the strongest combination available, because it means the site is being shown more often, ranked higher, and clicked more when it appears. That is the outcome the redirect mapping was built to protect, and it is the part of a rebuild that most often goes wrong. It did not. Your own brand searches gained ground, and several of the newly published facility pages picked up their first impressions and clicks within the month, which is fast for pages that did not exist in July. The exception worth naming: a few older variations of your brand name lost clicks and slipped in position while Google re-crawls the replaced pages. That is expected during a rebuild and is the thing to watch rather than act on.
